Calcium Indicators

By Howard Wang and Ting-Ruei Liu

ECE 587 · Extra Credit Presentation II

Why calcium tracks spiking

  • A spike opens voltage-gated Ca2+ channels
  • Ca2+ inside a resting neuron is very low: 50–100 nM
  • Outside the cell it is 1–2 mM, about 10,000 times higher
  • Activity raises it 10 to 100 times

Numbers from Grienberger & Konnerth (2012).

How GCaMP works

  • One protein with three parts: M13 peptide, cpGFP and calmodulin (CaM)
  • cpGFP is GFP cut open next to its chromophore
  • Ca2+ binds CaM, CaM grabs M13, and the opening closes
  • The chromophore turns bright, and dims again when Ca2+ leaves

jGCaMP8 with Ca2+ bound (orange). This version uses an ENOSP peptide in place of M13.

Zhang et al. (2023), Fig. 1a. CC BY.

Limit: photobleaching

  • Excitation light slowly destroys the fluorophore, so brightness drops
  • F0 drifts, so ΔF/F needs a running baseline
  • More laser power gives more signal but a shorter experiment

jGCaMP8 sensors lost 13–28% of their fluorescence in 10 minutes of illumination.

Zhang et al. (2023), Extended Data Fig. 4b. CC BY.

Calcium is slow

  • A spike lasts about 1 ms
  • jGCaMP8 rises in 2–4 ms but takes 45–190 ms to decay halfway
  • GCaMP6 is slower: 50–150 ms to reach its peak
  • Spikes close together merge into one transient

Zhang et al. (2023), Fig. 4e; Chen et al. (2013).

Color: fluorescence. Gray ticks: spikes. The numbers count the spikes in each burst.

From fluorescence back to spikes

  • Spike inference tries to recover the spike train from ΔF/F
  • Methods: deconvolution (OASIS), model fitting (MLspike), trained networks (CASCADE)
  • A single spike is detected only about 10% of the time
  • The result is best read as a firing rate

Detection probability against number of spikes, in two GCaMP6f mouse lines.

Biological concerns

  • GCaMP binds Ca2+, so it buffers the signal it measures
  • Too much expression fills the nucleus and stunts neurites
  • Expression from early life can cause epileptiform activity in some mouse lines
  • Use low expression and validated lines

Also Tian et al. (2009); Gasterstädt et al. (2020).

The engineering tradeoff

  • Each GCaMP generation has sensitive (s), medium (m) and fast (f) versions
  • Sensitive: bigger signal per spike, slower decay
  • Fast: follows spike trains, smaller signal
  • Tighter Ca2+ binding gives more signal but slower release

Chen et al. (2013); Zhang et al. (2023).

Summary

  • Spikes let Ca2+ into the cell, and GCaMP turns that Ca2+ into green light
  • The signal is slow and nonlinear, so it shows firing rate, not exact spike times
  • Bleaching, buffering and expression level all have to be controlled
